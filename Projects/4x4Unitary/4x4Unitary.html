Welcome to my integrated photonics CNOT gate project! This was a group project focused on the design of a silicon nitride photonic CNOT gate for linear optical quantum computing. The aim of the project was to design a proof-of-concept integrated quantum logic gate using on-chip photonic components, including grating couplers, multimode interferometers, and Mach-Zehnder interferometers.

The project was based around a 4×4 unitary optical circuit operating at a wavelength of 780 nm. This wavelength was chosen because silicon nitride is well suited to visible and near-visible photonics, and because the 780 nm region is highly relevant to quantum photonic technologies. The overall design used dual-rail encoding, where the logical quantum states are represented by photons travelling through different waveguides.

My main contribution to the project was the design and optimisation of the grating couplers. These components allow light to be coupled vertically between an optical fibre and the photonic chip, making them essential for getting light into and out of the device. I used Ansys Lumerical FDTD to model the grating coupler, beginning from an application-gallery structure and then modifying the parameters.

The optimisation process involved moving from 2D simulations to more realistic 3D simulations. In 2D, I used parameter sweeps and optimisation procedures to identify promising values for the grating design. However, when moving into 3D, the initial efficiency values were extremely low, which showed that the issue was not only the numerical parameters, but also the physical geometry of the grating. To solve this, I redesigned the grating from a simple rectangular structure into a wider, curved, cone-shaped grating with a taper between the grating region and the waveguide. This created a smoother transition for the optical mode and significantly improved the coupling performance. The final grating design achieved a simulated coupling efficiency (Figure of Merit) of approximately 98.5%

The wider project also included the design of 50:50 MMIs, 33:66 splitting structures, and Mach-Zehnder interferometers for phase control. Together, these components formed the basis of a post-selected linear optical CNOT gate. The project was limited to on-chip photonic design, so off-chip propagation and full experimental implementation were not considered.

Looking back, this project was one of my first major experiences working with integrated quantum photonics and professional photonic simulation software. It helped me understand how difficult it is to move from theory to fabrication. It also showed me how strongly optical performance depends on fabrication constraints, geometry, material choice, and simulation setup.

I am proud of this project because I achieved a successful simulation-based design of a complex photonic component. It gave me valuable experience applying coupled-mode theory to a practical photonic design, while also developing my skills with photonic simulation software.






Download Grating Coupler Notes PDF

This document contains a set of personal development notes that I created while designing the grating coupler for the photonic CNOT gate project. These notes were mainly written for myself during the development process, rather than as a final polished report.

They show how I approached the design step by step, including setting up the initial grating coupler model, choosing materials, defining the 780 nm wavelength, estimating starting parameters, and running early 2D simulation sweeps. The notes also include my reasoning behind key design choices, such as fibre angle, pitch, duty cycle, etch depth, fibre position, and the transition towards a wider cone-shaped grating structure.

I have included them here to give a more honest view of the development process behind the final design, including the assumptions, testing, and practical simulation work that went into building the grating coupler.
